LOOOP is a sustainable retail concept built around the idea that fashion can create connections between **people, designers, and the planet**.

The project explores how a retail space can move away from traditional linear layouts and instead use **curved forms, circular movement, and styled displays** to create a more connected shopping experience.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/looop1.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

## The Core Idea

### Straight lines divide. Curves unite.

The visual and spatial concept for LOOOP began with a simple observation: straight lines naturally create boundaries, while curved lines bend, circle, overlap, and bring elements together.

This became the foundation for both the brand identity and the showroom itself.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/looop2.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

## Concept & Brand Direction

The name **LOOOP** reflects the project's focus on circularity and connection. Its overlapping circular forms represent an ongoing relationship between **people, design, and the environment**.

The concept was developed around three connected ideas:

<div class="row mt-4">
  <div class="col-md-4">
    <h4>Store Vision</h4>
    <p>
      Create a space using rounded furniture and displays arranged through curved lines,
      allowing designers, garments, and customers to meet and interact naturally.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="col-md-4">
    <h4>Design Concept</h4>
    <p>
      Use styled mannequins more prominently than conventional racks or shelves,
      allowing pieces from different designers to be combined and presented together.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="col-md-4">
    <h4>Values & Mission</h4>
    <p>
      Build a loop between people, design, and the planet through sustainability,
      creativity, community, and circular fashion.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## Mood & Visual Direction

The mood board brought together references from **fashion, retail interiors, organic architecture, circular forms, and sustainability**.

Rather than treating sustainability only as an environmental message, the visual direction connects it with expressive fashion and contemporary design.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/looop4.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

The recurring circular forms became an important part of the visual language — appearing in the logo, graphic elements, furniture, floor plan, and architectural references.

## Designing the Showroom

The showroom layout translates the idea of **“curves unite”** into the physical customer experience.

Instead of filling the space with rows of conventional racks, the concept uses mannequins, rounded seating, open areas, and curved visual elements to encourage movement around the room.

<div class="row mt-4">
  <div class="col-md-6">
    {% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/looop6.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}
  </div>
  <div class="col-md-6">
    {% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/looop7.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}
  </div>

The mannequins act as the primary fashion displays, allowing garments from different designers to be styled together rather than separating each collection into rigid sections.

A central seating area creates another loop within the space, giving visitors a place to pause while maintaining an open circulation path around it.

## Bringing the Space to Life

The floor plan was then developed into a **virtual showroom**, translating the curved spatial concept into a three-dimensional retail environment.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/looop8.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

Warm wood surfaces and organic architectural forms contrast with the green flooring, while curved windows, furniture, and display areas reinforce the original concept.

The result is intended to feel less like a conventional store divided into product sections and more like a shared environment where different designers and pieces can coexist.

## Promotional Concept

The final promotional material connects the showroom back to LOOOP's sustainability message:
